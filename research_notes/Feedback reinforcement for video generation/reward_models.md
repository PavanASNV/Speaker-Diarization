## Q1. Which video and image reward models lead in 2025–2026 (architecture, training data, dimensions, agreement with humans, availability)?

### Takeaway
Open video reward models (RMs) have converged on fine-tuned Qwen-VL-family VLMs (2B–8B, some to 72B) that emit multi-dimensional pointwise scores (VideoReward: visual/motion/text; VideoScore2: visual/text/physics) or pairwise chain-of-thought judgments (UnifiedReward-Think, VR-Thinker), increasingly trained SFT→GRPO; 2026 work moves to rubric/checklist-grounded, decoupled-reasoning, calibrated-distributional and personalised RMs. No public RM has an explicit "photographic vs 3D-render/CGI look" axis, and frontier labs (Seedance, HunyuanVideo, SkyReels) run separate specialist RMs for alignment/structure, motion and aesthetics — with motion consistently the hardest dimension, needing dedicated paired data.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Video RMs / evaluators with verified details (chronological)
- **VideoScore (TIGER-Lab, Jun 2024, EMNLP 2024)**: base Mantis-8B-Idefics2; trained on VideoFeedback = 37.6K text-to-video pairs from 11 generators (plus some real videos as augmentation) human-rated on 5 aspects (Visual Quality, Temporal Consistency, Dynamic Degree, Text-to-Video Alignment, Factual Consistency); Spearman 77.1 with humans on VideoFeedback-test, "beating the prior best metrics by about 50 points"; VideoScore-Bench ≈7,000 videos drawn from VideoFeedback, EvalCrafter, GenAI-Bench and VBench (Likert + preference); v1.1 (Nov 2024) improves the text-alignment sub-score and accepts 48 frames. — [VideoScore GitHub](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/VideoScore)
- **VideoScore2 (TIGER-Lab, arXiv 2509.22799, Sep 2025; released 1 Oct 2025)**: three axes — visual quality, text alignment, physical/common-sense consistency — with chain-of-thought analysis written *before* scores; data VideoFeedback2 = 2,933 prompts, 27,168 videos from >20 T2V models, 81,504 scores with rationales; SFT then GRPO; 500-example split = VideoScore-Bench-v2; reports 44.35 (+5.94) accuracy in-domain and 50.37 (+4.32) average over four OOD benchmarks; removing CoT drops in-domain accuracy 39.81 → 32.17 (search summary of paper). — [arXiv 2509.22799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22799); [HF demo](https://tiger-ai-lab-videoscore2.hf.space)
- VideoScore2 code: SFT on LLaMA-Factory, RL on the Video-R1 framework; its harness evaluates 11 baselines (AIGVE-MACS, DeQA-Score, DOVER, ImageReward, Q-Align, Q-Insight, UnifiedReward, VideoPhy2-auto-eval, VideoReward, VisionReward, VideoScore1) on VideoScore-Bench-v2 plus OOD VideoGen-RewardBench, T2VQA-DB (converted to pairwise), MJ-Bench-Video, VideoPhy2-test, and MLLM-prompting baselines via OpenRouter (example `openai/gpt-5-mini`); default inference samples frames at `infer_fps` = 2.0. — [VideoScore2 GitHub](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/VideoScore2); [VideoScore2 eval README](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/VideoScore2/blob/HEAD/eval/README.md)
- **VideoAlign / VideoReward (Kuaishou/Kling team et al., arXiv 2501.13918, Jan 2025)**: base Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct; three heads Visual Quality (VQ), Motion Quality (MQ), Text Alignment (TA); repo MIT-licensed, checkpoints `KwaiVGI/VideoReward`; the paper also introduces VideoGen-RewardBench and the Flow-DPO / Flow-RWR / Flow-NRG alignment algorithms. — [VideoAlign GitHub](https://github.com/KwaiVGI/VideoAlign); [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- VideoReward is trained with a Bradley–Terry-with-Ties (BTT) loss that keeps tie labels instead of discarding them; a secondary summary (not verified against the paper) gives 61.26% overall VideoGen-RewardBench accuracy *with ties* vs 56.77% for VisionReward. — [VideoAlign arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13918v2); [secondary summary (besthub.dev)](https://www.besthub.dev/articles/how-human-feedback-supercharges-video-generation-the-videoalign-pipeline-explained-bf305cbaeec4)
- VideoReward outputs are standardised: DanceGRPO de-normalises the VQ head as `VQ * 2.2476 + 3.6757` per the model's `model_config.json` (i.e., raw outputs are z-scores against its training distribution, and each head has its own scale). — [DanceGRPO GitHub](https://github.com/XueZeyue/DanceGRPO)
- **VisionReward (THUDM/Zhipu, arXiv 2412.21059, Dec 2024; AAAI 2026)**: decomposes preference into binary checklist questions answered by a fine-tuned VLM, then a linear weighted sum (weights shipped as `weight.json`) produces the score; also a direct two-video compare mode; VisionReward-Image base cogvlm2-llama3-chat-19B, VisionReward-Video base cogvlm2-video-llama3-chat; VisionRewardDB-Image 48K × 60 dimensions, VisionRewardDB-Video 33K × 64 dimensions; 64.0 (Tau) / 72.1 (Diff) on its Video Preference Test Set, "surpassing VideoScore by 17.2%"; proposes Multi-objective Preference Optimization (MPO) to balance dimensions during RLHF. — [VisionReward GitHub](https://github.com/zai-org/VisionReward)
- VisionReward details by version (search summary): video checklist = 9 dimensions, 20 sub-dimensions, 64 binary questions; v1 data 48k images + 33k videos, v3 describes 81K samples and 5M binary annotations; T2V models optimised with VisionReward reach a 31.6% higher pairwise win rate than with VideoScore. — [arXiv 2412.21059v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21059v3); [VisionReward-Video model card](https://huggingface.co/THUDM/VisionReward-Video/blob/main/README.md)
- **UnifiedReward family (CodeGoat24/UnifiedReward team, also authors of Pref-GRPO; arXiv 2503.05236, Mar 2025)**: "first unified reward model for multimodal understanding and generation", pairwise ranking + pointwise scoring for images and videos; Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA bases; training data mixes pairwise/pointwise sets incl. ShareGPTVideo, VideoDPO and VideoFeedback; ships eval code for GenAI-Bench-Video/Image, VideoGen-RewardBench and VL-RewardBench. Releases: UnifiedReward-2.0 (25 Sep 2025; Qwen 3B/7B/32B/72B) adds pairwise and pointwise scoring of image *and video* generation on **Alignment, Coherence (/Physics), Style**; Qwen3-VL 2B/4B/8B variants (Nov 2025); UnifiedReward-Edit (Oct 2025); UnifiedReward-Flex personalised RM with UnifiedReward-Flex-SFT-90K data (Feb 2026); Qwen3.5-based ("qwen35") 2.0/Edit/Think/Flex (Mar 2026). — [UnifiedReward GitHub](https://github.com/CodeGoat24/UnifiedReward)
- **UnifiedReward-Think (arXiv 2505.03318, NeurIPS 2025)**: first unified multimodal CoT RM; cold start distils GPT-4o reasoning on ImageGen-CoT-Reward-5K, then rejection sampling on correctly-judged samples, then GRPO on incorrectly-judged samples with format + accuracy rewards. — [arXiv 2505.03318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03318); [UnifiedReward-Think README](https://github.com/CodeGoat24/UnifiedReward/blob/HEAD/UnifiedReward-Think/README.md); [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117965)
- **VR-Thinker (Kling Team, CUHK MMLab, NJU; arXiv 2510.10518, Oct 2025)**: "thinking-with-image" video RM that calls a frame-selection operation during reasoning with a configurable visual-memory window and can request more frames when unsure; motivated by (i) visual tokens exhausting context and (ii) hallucination when all frames are packed into the first prompt; trained cold-start visual CoT → rejection-sampling FT → GRPO; the 7B model reports 80.5% on VideoGen-RewardBench, 82.3% on GenAI-Bench, 75.6% on MJ-Bench-Video (third-party review; unverified), at the cost of higher latency. — [arXiv 2510.10518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10518v1); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/vr-thinker-boosting-video-reward-models-through-thinking-with-image-reasoning); [TLDR summary](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2510.10518)
- **RewardDance (ByteDance Seed, arXiv 2509.08826, Sep 2025)**: generative reward = probability of a "yes" token that a generation beats a reference under stated criteria; InternVL-based RMs scaled 1B → 26B plus richer reward context (instructions, reference examples, CoT); quality under RL and test-time scaling improves with RM size, and larger RMs resist reward hacking (26B keeps high reward variance late in RL); tested on T2I, T2V and I2V with FLUX.1-dev, Seedream-3.0 and Seedance-1.0; a light pointwise variant drives "Search over Paths" trajectory pruning (search summaries). — [arXiv 2509.08826](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08826v1); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/rewarddance-reward-scaling-in-visual-generation)
- **LiFT (Fudan FUXI, arXiv 2412.04814, Dec 2024)**: LiFT-HRA human ratings *with rationales* (10K/20K releases); LiFT-Critic 13B and 40B LoRA (v1.0, v1.5); reward-weighted fine-tuning of CogVideoX-2B makes it outperform CogVideoX-5B "across all 16 metrics". — [LiFT GitHub](https://github.com/CodeGoat24/LiFT)
- **VideoPhy-2 AutoEval (arXiv 2503.06800, Mar 2025; ICLR 2026)**: one multi-task auto-rater outputs semantic adherence (1–5), physical commonsense (1–5) and per-physical-rule classification (0 violation / 1 adherence / 2 not groundable); benchmark "joint" score = fraction with SA ≥ 4 and PC ≥ 4; checkpoint public on HF. — [VideoPhy-2 README](https://github.com/Hritikbansal/videophy/blob/HEAD/VIDEOPHY2/README.md)
- **Quality-assessment lineage**: Q-Align teaches LMMs to score via discrete text-defined levels; its OneAlign checkpoint scores image quality, image aesthetics and video quality. — [Q-Align GitHub](https://github.com/Q-Future/Q-Align). VisualQuality-R1 (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight) is a Qwen2.5-VL no-reference IQA model trained by "reinforcement learning to rank", outputting reasoning plus a score. — [VisualQuality-R1 GitHub](https://github.com/TianheWu/VisualQuality-R1). VQ-Insight (2506.18564; progressive visual RL for a quality VLM), T2VScore (2401.07781; alignment + mixture-of-experts quality), DOVER/FAST-VQA (2022; aesthetic/technical VQA) are listed as the evaluator lineage. — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **Aligning Human Sense (arXiv 2608.21425, Aug 2026; ECCV 2026)**: argues scalar RMs flatten multi-dimensional preference; "elite-guided" filtering splits annotations into high/low-consistency subsets, trains an Elite Scorer on the high-consistency subset, replaces low-consistency vote scores with calibrated continuous rewards, then trains the final RM; models each dimension as a reward *distribution* aligned with Wasserstein distance and applies distributional alignment inside GRPO; code trains per-dimension Qwen2-VL-2B RMs on Video-Bench annotations (action, aesthetic_quality, color, imaging_quality, motion_effects, object_class, scene, temporal_consistency, video_text_consistency). — [arXiv 2608.21425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21425); [ECCV 2026 poster](https://eccv.ecva.net/virtual/2026/poster/3296); [AHS GitHub](https://github.com/alignhs26/ahs)
- **SoliReward (arXiv 2512.22170, Dec 2025)**: targets reward hacking and annotation noise in video RMs; argues VideoAlign-style BTT (keeping all tie samples in a 3-way loss) is ill-suited to its annotation scheme and finds a BT variant trained on win-lose *and win-tie* pairs gives higher RM accuracy than BTT (different data; not a refutation of VideoAlign) (search summary). — [arXiv 2512.22170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22170)

### Inferences
- **Adopt-now shortlist for the app (open weights, video-native):** (1) VideoReward 2B (MIT; VQ/MQ/TA; small enough to score every take; used as the video reward in DanceGRPO's open training scripts); (2) VideoScore2 (adds a physics/common-sense axis and rationales useful for debugging); (3) UnifiedReward-2.0 / -Think on Qwen3-VL-8B or Qwen3.5 (native pairwise video comparison; Alignment/Coherence-Physics/Style); (4) VideoPhy-2 AutoEval (physics, with rule-level output); (5) HPSv3 on 3–5 keyframes per take (aesthetics/photo prior; HPDv3 contains real photographs); (6) VQAScore with a modern Qwen backend for fine-grained prompt-element checks. Licenses must be checked per checkpoint (only VideoAlign's MIT was confirmed).
- **Evaluate next:** VR-Thinker (best reported pairwise accuracy, but slower); VisionReward-Video (interpretable checklist template — closest structural match to the app's rubric, but CogVLM2-based and older); UnifiedReward-Flex (personalised criteria — closest open analogue to user-specific weights); EvalVerse and AesRM (cinematic/aesthetic expert preferences — potentially closest to "live-action cinematic" taste; availability unverified).
- **Mapping to the app's 7 dimensions:** prompt alignment ← VideoReward-TA, VideoScore2-TA, UnifiedReward-Alignment, VQAScore; photorealism/anti-CGI ← *no off-the-shelf RM* (see Q3) — needs a custom classifier plus HPSv3 photo prior; composition vs storyboard ← detector IoU (Q3) + Claude; identity ← face/body embeddings (Q3); motion ← VideoReward-MQ + flow/flicker metrics; surreal integration ← Claude rubric (physics RMs will penalise intentional violations); aesthetic ← HPSv3 / Q-Align aesthetic / AesRM.
- Frontier labs' pattern (separate alignment/structure, motion, aesthetic RMs; motion fixed with controlled pairs + DPO) mirrors the app's multi-dimension design and suggests the app should collect occasional *controlled* pairwise labels ("same prompt, which motion is better?") rather than relying only on holistic star ratings, which conflate dimensions.
- All public RMs were trained on 2024–2025 generators (VideoFeedback: 11 models; VideoFeedback2: >20 models); their ability to discriminate among Seedance 2.0 / Veo 3.1 / Kling 3.0 / Sora 2 takes is unknown and may be compressed at the top of their range — validate on the app's own ratings before trusting them.

## Q2. How reliable is VLM-as-judge (GPT / Claude / Gemini / Qwen-VL) for video quality, physics, temporal consistency and text alignment — biases and reliability techniques?

### Takeaway
Prompted general VLMs are mediocre video judges: on GenAI-Arena's 1,069 human video votes, 2024-era GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro reach only ~46–48% 4-way accuracy (random 25%), and MLLM-judge correlation with humans is high for static attributes (~0.70–0.75) but low for temporal consistency (0.40) and motion (0.51); trained, reasoning RMs (VR-Thinker, VideoScore2) do markedly better. Reliability improves with reasoning-before-scoring, rubric/checklist decomposition, pairwise comparison with ties, adaptive frame selection and larger judges, while known risks are linguistic-plausibility bias, positional bias, bias from seeing generation traces, and over-penalising brief frame artifacts. No benchmark evidence specific to Claude as a video judge was found.

### Cited Findings
- **GenAI-Bench (GenAI-Arena, TIGER-Lab, 2024)** benchmarks MLLMs as judges against cleaned human votes: 1,735 image-generation, 919 image-editing and 1,069 video-generation votes; 4-way labels (A>B, B>A, A=B=Good, A=B=Bad). Video-generation accuracy: GPT-4o 48.46, Gemini-1.5-Pro 46.21, LLaVA 30.4, CogVLM 24.6, BLIP-2 16.93, Idefics2 16.46, MiniCPM-V-2.5 6.55, Qwen-VL 2.15, VideoLLaVA 0.0 (random 25.16); Claude is listed only as a TODO. — [GenAI-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/GenAI-Bench)
- Trained video RMs report far higher pairwise accuracy on related benchmarks (VR-Thinker 7B: 82.3% GenAI-Bench, 80.5% VideoGen-RewardBench, 75.6% MJ-Bench-Video; third-party review, metric definitions may differ from the 4-way GenAI-Bench protocol). — [Moonlight review of VR-Thinker](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/vr-thinker-boosting-video-reward-models-through-thinking-with-image-reasoning)
- **MLLM-as-a-Judge (arXiv 2402.04788, Feb 2024; image tasks)**: GPT-4V reaches roughly 70% agreement with humans overall, ~79% in pairwise comparison, but only Pearson 0.490 in absolute scoring (search summary); the benchmark covers three judging modes — scoring, pair comparison, batch ranking. — [alphaXiv abstract](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.04788); [MLLM-Judge GitHub](https://github.com/Dongping-Chen/MLLM-Judge)
- **Video-Bench (arXiv 2504.04907, Apr 2025)**: MLLM-based evaluation with few-shot scoring and "chain-of-query"; correlation with human ratings by dimension: imaging quality 0.733, aesthetic 0.702, **temporal consistency 0.402, motion effects 0.514**, video-text consistency 0.732, action 0.718, object class 0.735, color 0.750, scene 0.733 — vs VBench's classical metrics MUSIQ 0.363 (imaging), LAION aesthetic 0.446, CLIP 0.260 (temporal), RAFT/AMT 0.329 (motion), ViCLIP 0.445 (video-text), UMT 0.411 (action), GRiT 0.469/0.545 (object/color), Tag2Text 0.422 (scene). — [Video-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/Video-Bench/Video-Bench)
- Video-Bench also reports poor *human–human* agreement on semantic-consistency dimensions, partly because raters reward visual appeal over prompt fidelity (search summary). — [Video-Bench arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04907)
- **Co-Director (arXiv 2604.24842, Apr 2026; agentic generative video storytelling)**: MLLM–human alignment is near the human–human ceiling on narrative dimensions but weaker on visual quality, attributed to the MLLM penalising brief frame-level artifacts that humans barely notice (search summary). — [Co-Director arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24842)
- **Reasoning before scoring helps**: VideoScore2's in-domain accuracy falls from 39.81 to 32.17 without CoT (search summary). — [VideoScore2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22799)
- **Frame-sampling/context blind spots**: VR-Thinker attributes RM errors to visual inputs consuming context budget and to hallucination when all frames are packed into the initial prompt, and fixes it with on-demand frame selection and a visual memory window. — [VR-Thinker arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10518v1). HunyuanVideo 1.5's team found existing RMs "struggle to distinguish fine-grained motion quality" and used offline DPO for motion instead. — [HunyuanVideo 1.5 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18870v1)
- **Bias findings (2026)**: MLLM judges favour linguistic plausibility over visual correctness ("Perceptual Judgment Bias", arXiv 2606.02578). — [arXiv 2606.02578](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02578v1). MM-JudgeBias (ACL 2026) benchmarks compositional biases across 26 MLLM judges (image-based). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1162.pdf). A 2026 study systematically evaluates positional bias when MLLMs process multiple videos (multi-video summarisation setting; title-level). — [arXiv 2606.04596](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04596). "Don't Read the Log" (2609.28564) shows execution traces can bias multimodal verifiers. — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **Pointwise vs pairwise**: Pref-GRPO shows pointwise RM scores cluster tightly within a group of samples, so normalisation inflates tiny differences into large "illusory advantages" that drive reward hacking; using pairwise win rates within the group as the reward gives more stable, directional signals. — [Pref-GRPO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20751v1); [Pref-GRPO project page](https://codegoat24.github.io/UnifiedReward/Pref-GRPO)
- **Judge scale**: RewardDance reports that larger generative judges (to 26B) consistently improve downstream quality and reduce reward hacking. — [RewardDance arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08826v1)
- **Rubric/checklist trend**: VisionReward replaces holistic scores with 64 binary video questions + learned linear weights ([VisionReward GitHub](https://github.com/zai-org/VisionReward)); Rubrics-as-Rewards (2507.17746), OpenRubrics (2510.07743), RubricRL (2511.20651), RAVEN-Eval (rubric-guided pairwise LMM judgment, 2608.09111), VideoArgus (agentic rubric-grounded evaluation, 2608.05485), RewardVerse (query-adaptive rubrics, 2609.22947) and FIRM-Video ("check before you score", 2608.21839) all push instance-specific rubrics/checks before scoring. — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **Hierarchical judging for physics**: PhyGenEval evaluates key physical phenomena first, then their order, then overall naturalness, and its GPT-4o variant is described as "highly consistent with human feedback". — [PhyGenBench GitHub](https://github.com/OpenGVLab/PhyGenBench)
- **Meta-evaluation resources for judges**: UVE-Bench (2503.09949; pairwise preferences across 15 AIGV aspects, meta-evaluates MLLM judges); VF-Eval (2505.23693; MLLM feedback on AIGC videos); JudgeAnything (2503.17489); SLVMEval (2603.29186; synthetic meta-eval for long-video judges, 10 aspects); WorldJen (2605.03475; VLM judge validated against blind human Bradley–Terry ratings); JudgeFit (2606.22918; per-VLM physical-taxonomy discovery). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation). AVENUE (2609.04253) shows a rigorous protocol: judges vs 13 human evaluators using pairwise agreement, leave-one-out agreement and Kendall τ-b. — [AVENUE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04253)
- **Test-time selection depends on selector reliability**: a 2026 compute-value audit of test-time scaling for video world models finds larger candidate pools raise oracle quality but selectors fail to recover it once costs are counted ("Sampling headroom is not selection gain", 2609.13257); an LLM analogue reports headroom capture tracks the verifier's correlation with true quality. — [arXiv 2609.13257](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13257); [alphaXiv 2608.18931](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18931); [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)

### Inferences
- The app's Claude judge is likely most trustworthy on static, frame-visible dimensions (prompt alignment, composition, aesthetic, photographic look) and least trustworthy on motion quality and temporal identity drift; those should be co-scored by specialist RMs/metrics (Q3) and given lower weight unless Claude is shown temporal evidence.
- Concrete judge-protocol upgrades: (1) score *pairwise within a shot* (all takes of the same shot, both A/B orders, tie option "both good/both bad"), converting to Bradley–Terry/Elo per shot, instead of absolute 1–10 per take; (2) require evidence-then-score per dimension using a fixed checklist of binary questions (VisionReward/FIRM-Video style), e.g., "any plastic/subsurface-less skin?", "any CG-style specular highlights?", "does the subject's bounding region match storyboard box X?"; (3) send ≥2 fps frames with timestamps plus a motion aid (frame-difference or optical-flow strip, or a short-interval frame triplet around fast action) and allow a second round in which Claude requests specific frames (VR-Thinker pattern); (4) hide the planner's reasoning, prompt-authorship and model identity from the judge to limit self-preference/trace bias; (5) temperature 0 / multiple samples with majority or mean, and an ensemble with at least one non-Claude judge (e.g., UnifiedReward-Think) to decorrelate errors.
- Expect absolute Claude scores to be poorly calibrated (MLLM-as-a-Judge: Pearson ~0.49 for scoring vs ~79% pairwise agreement); calibrate per dimension against star ratings (isotonic/ordinal regression) or use only rankings.
- Because human–human agreement itself is limited on semantic dimensions (Video-Bench), target judge–user agreement should be measured against a per-user noise ceiling (e.g., re-rating consistency) rather than 100%.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark numbers for Claude (any version) as a video judge were found; GenAI-Bench lists Claude as TODO. Also no 2026 numbers for GPT-5/Gemini 3-class judges on VideoGen-RewardBench or VideoScore-Bench-v2 could be retrieved (VideoScore2 runs `gpt-5-mini` baselines but tables were unreachable).
- Self-preference bias (a model favouring outputs of prompts it wrote) has no video-specific study in the sources found; "Don't Read the Log" is the closest evidence (title-level).
- Verbosity/length bias and position bias specifically for *pairwise video judging* were not quantified in retrieved sources.

## Q3. Specialised signals: photographic vs 3D-render/CGI detection, identity consistency across shots, motion smoothness / flicker, physics evaluators, composition/layout adherence

### Takeaway
Physics, motion and identity now each have dedicated evaluators or reward models (VideoPhy-2 AutoEval, PhyGenEval, VBench-2.0 physics/human-fidelity, VMBench, TRAJAN, Identity-GRPO, latent identity RMs), and layout can be checked with detection/tracking pipelines (T2V-CompBench, GenEval-style); but there is no validated off-the-shelf "photographic vs 3D-render/CGI/cartoon look" scorer — AI-generated-media detectors (DeMamba, B-Free) answer a different question (real vs generated), so this axis needs a custom classifier. Classical temporal metrics (flow, frame interpolation, CLIP) correlate weakly with human motion judgments (≈0.26–0.33).

### Cited Findings

#### Photorealism / anti-CGI look
- AI-generated video detection: DeMamba introduces the million-scale GenVideo benchmark (real: Kinetics-400 260,232 clips, Youku-mPLUG 953,279, MSR-VTT; fake: many generators, e.g., ZeroScope 133,169, I2VGen-XL 61,975); a lighter GenVideo-100K (10,000 samples per forgery category) was released Sep 2025; its detector passed a CAICT evaluation with the highest rating (Dec 2024). — [DeMamba GitHub](https://github.com/chenhaoxing/DeMamba)
- B-Free (CVPR 2025) trains AI-generated *image* detectors bias-free by generating fakes as self-conditioned reconstructions of real images (so differences come only from generator artifacts), using a DINOv2-pretrained ViT with registers and content augmentation, giving better generalisation, robustness and calibration. — [B-Free GitHub](https://github.com/grip-unina/B-Free)
- HPSv3's training data deliberately includes high- and low-quality *real photographs* alongside SOTA generations, giving it a learned prior over photographic images. — [HPSv3 GitHub](https://github.com/MizzenAI/HPSv3)
- Generic VBench quality metrics track humans weakly: MUSIQ imaging quality 0.363, LAION aesthetic 0.446 correlation, vs 0.733 / 0.702 for an MLLM-based evaluator. — [Video-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/Video-Bench/Video-Bench)
- Style-specific RMs exist for the opposite domain (anime): "Aligning Anime Video Generation with Human Feedback" (2504.10044) and AniMatrix (2605.03652; anime reward model, "thinks in art, not physics"). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- VQA-style probability scoring (VQAScore: image-to-text P("yes")) and RewardDance's P("yes") reward show that a judge can be turned into a calibrated binary-question scorer for any visual property. — [t2v_metrics GitHub](https://github.com/linzhiqiu/t2v_metrics); [RewardDance arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08826v1)

#### Identity consistency
- Identity rewards for video: "Identity-Preserving I2V via Reward-Guided Optimization" (2510.14255; facial-identity reward); Identity-GRPO (2510.14256; multi-human identity reward + GRPO); a latent-space identity RM trained on ~27.5K human comparisons for multi-subject customisation under complex motion (2603.12257); PersonalVideo (2411.17048; differentiable identity and semantic-consistency rewards); ID-Crafter (2511.00511; VLM-grounded online RL for multi-subject generation); InfLVG (2505.17574; composite verifier of ArcFace + CLIP + Qwen2.5-VL as GRPO reward for long-video consistency); AESR (2608.20749; VLM locates faulty segments, frame-level repair, MoE selection — a black-box wrapper). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- VBench-2.0 provides Human_Identity, Human_Anatomy and Human_Clothes dimensions that can score arbitrary user videos, plus a human-anomaly detection pipeline (ViTDetector, Apr 2025). — [VBench-2.0 README](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench/blob/HEAD/VBench-2.0/README.md)
- OpenS2V-Eval (subject-to-video) proposes three new automatic metrics "aligned with human perception" (works using it report Face Similarity and Naturalness), v1.1 adds a Motion Smoothness score; ConsisID-Bench has 150 human-domain subject images × 90 prompts. — [OpenS2V-Nexus GitHub](https://github.com/PKU-YuanGroup/OpenS2V-Nexus)
- Multi-shot/story evaluation: MSVBench (2602.23969; multi-shot evaluation with a distilled judge), MSAVBench (2605.20183), LoCoT2V-Bench (2510.26412; long-form multi-scene T2V), MSG Score (2411.19121; hierarchical verifier for multi-scene generate-and-select, distilled to a light student). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)

#### Motion smoothness, flicker, temporal consistency
- VBench's dimensions include subject_consistency, background_consistency, temporal_flickering, motion_smoothness, dynamic_degree, aesthetic_quality, imaging_quality, spatial_relationship etc., implemented with AMT, UMT, RAM, CLIP, RAFT, GRiT and others. — [VBench GitHub](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench)
- Human alignment of those classical signals is weak: CLIP temporal consistency 0.260; RAFT and AMT motion 0.329 (vs MLLM 0.402 / 0.514). — [Video-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/Video-Bench/Video-Bench)

#### Composition / layout adherence
- T2V-CompBench evaluates compositional T2V in 7 categories using three evaluator families: MLLM-based (attribute binding), detection-based (spatial relationships) and tracking-based (motion binding). — [T2V-CompBench GitHub](https://github.com/KaiyueSun98/T2V-CompBench)
- Flow-GRPO uses the detection-based GenEval reward to verify object counts in RL (with CLIP similarity for edit consistency). — [Flow-GRPO GitHub](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo)
- Layout/camera-specific checks (curated list, title-level): SG-PVR (2606.11838; scene-graph plan-and-verify reranking); "Beyond Trial-and-Error" (2608.12290; scene-graph and artifact checks drive prompt/seed/guidance optimisation for I2V adherence); VGIF-Bench (2607.13527; spatio-temporal instruction following); KeyFrame-Compass (2607.14202; multi-keyframe adherence, six metrics); Stable Cinemetrics/SCINE (2509.26555; filmmaking taxonomy with distilled VLM evaluator); camera rewards CamPilot (2601.16214), Geo-Align (2605.23903), CamVerse (2512.02870); DynamicEval (2510.07441; camera-motion pairwise preferences). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)

### Inferences
- **Anti-CGI/photographic-look scorer (highest-value custom component):** train a lightweight probe (logistic/MLP) on frozen DINOv2/SigLIP/CLIP features from 3–8 frames per take, positives = real film/photo stills and accepted live-action takes, negatives = 3D-render/CG/game-engine/anime/"AI-plastic" frames (including rejected takes the user flagged); add a VQAScore/Claude P("yes") probe ("Is this a frame from live-action footage shot on a real camera?") as a second opinion; use as a **gate** (veto) plus a continuous feature. Do not use AI-generation detectors for this — every hosted-model output is "generated", so they would saturate and track generator fingerprints, not look.
- **Identity across shots:** compute face embeddings (ArcFace-class) and body/outfit embeddings (person re-ID or DINOv2 on person crops) per take, compare against the character's canonical reference sheet *and* the last accepted shot; report min/median similarity over frames (catch mid-clip drift); tune thresholds per character from user ratings; keep Claude for coarse checks (hair, costume, age).
- **Motion:** combine VideoReward-MQ with cheap deterministic signals — RAFT-flow magnitude (penalise near-static takes when the shot plan calls for action), frame-difference flicker, interpolation-error smoothness — and a "too static" floor; note classical metrics correlate weakly with humans, so learn their weights from ratings rather than hand-setting.
- **Physics vs surreal intent:** physics RMs will score intentional surreal elements as violations; run them with a mask/instruction for planned violations (PhyWorldBench "anti-physics" framing), and score "surreal-element integration" as *physically consistent rendering of an impossible element* (lighting, shadows, contact, scale) via the Claude rubric.
- **Composition vs storyboard boxes:** run an open-vocabulary detector/segmenter on 3–5 keyframes, match detections to planned boxes by label, and score IoU, centroid offset and scale error (plus tracking for planned movement); this is deterministic, cheap and more reliable than a VLM estimating overlap.

### Gaps
- No validated, published "photographic vs CGI/3D-render" classifier for 2025–2026 generator outputs was found; DeMamba/B-Free accuracy numbers on modern hosted models (Veo/Kling/Seedance/Sora) were not retrieved.
- ArcFace/AdaFace similarity thresholds and correlation with human identity judgments in generated video were not retrieved (Identity-GRPO and related papers only at title level).
- TRAJAN, Video Consistency Distance, PhyJudge-9B and TRAVL accuracy vs humans not verified.
- No source found that evaluates layout adherence of hosted T2V models against explicit bounding-box storyboards.

## Q4. How should multiple rewards be combined and calibrated (weighted sums vs per-reward normalisation, Pareto/multi-objective, ensembles and uncertainty, Bradley–Terry with ties, calibrating weights to human ratings, personalised RMs)?

### Takeaway
Production video stacks still mostly use weighted linear combinations (Seedance "directly maximize a linear combination"; Flow-GRPO's weighted reward dict), but 2025–2026 evidence shows that summing before group normalisation collapses distinct reward combinations (GDPO, ICML 2026), pointwise scores create illusory advantages (Pref-GRPO), and noisy/ambiguous labels need tie-aware, uncertainty-aware or distributional losses (BTT, HPSv3 Gaussian loss, Aligning Human Sense). Best practice is: normalise each reward separately, prefer pairwise/relative signals, model ties and uncertainty, scale/ensemble judges to resist hacking, and learn weights (and per-user adjustments) from human comparisons.

### Cited Findings
- **Weighted sums in practice**: Seedance 1.0's refiner RLHF "directly maximize[s] a linear combination of these reward signals" from multiple RMs (search summary of report). — [Seedance 1.0 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09113v1). Flow-GRPO supports multi-reward training via a dict of reward→weight (example `"pickscore": 0.5, "aesthetic": 0.3`), "the final reward is a weighted sum of the individual rewards"; supported rewards include PickScore, GenEval, OCR, CLIPScore, Aesthetic, DeQA, ImageReward and UnifiedReward (served via sglang); Wan2.1 support added. — [Flow-GRPO GitHub](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo)
- **Per-reward normalisation (GDPO, NVIDIA/HKUST, arXiv 2601.05242, ICML 2026)**: applying GRPO to summed rewards causes "reward advantages collapse" — different reward combinations map to identical normalised advantages; GDPO normalises each reward separately within the group, sums, then applies batch-wise advantage normalisation so magnitude does not grow with the number of rewards; reward weights then set priorities; reported up to +2.7% accuracy and >4% better format compliance than GRPO on Qwen2.5-Instruct (LLM tasks; numbers via secondary reviews). — [GDPO GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/GDPO); [NVIDIA project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/twn/publication/icml_2026_gdpo/); [arXiv 2601.05242](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05242v1); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/gdpo-group-reward-decoupled-normalization-policy-optimization-for-multi-reward-rl-optimization)
- **DanceGRPO (arXiv 2505.07818, May 2025)**: open scripts train HunyuanVideo (T2V) and SkyReels-I2V with VideoAlign rewards (VQ reward curves de-normalised from z-scores), treat Wan-2.1 as an image model trained with HPS-v2.1, and use HPS-v2.1 for FLUX/Qwen-Image. — [DanceGRPO GitHub](https://github.com/XueZeyue/DanceGRPO)
- **Pairwise rewards against hacking (Pref-GRPO, arXiv 2508.20751, Aug 2025; v2 Apr 2026)**: pointwise scores cluster within groups → "illusory advantage" → over-optimisation and reward hacking (scores rise while quality degrades); replacing them with each sample's pairwise win rate within the group stabilises training; Feb 2026 release supports Wan2.2 and UnifiedReward-Flex-based Pref-GRPO for image and video. — [Pref-GRPO arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20751v1); [Pref-GRPO GitHub](https://github.com/CodeGoat24/Pref-GRPO)
- **Other multi-objective/credit methods (curated list, title-level)**: TaRoS (2511.19356; reward saturation and ranking collapse in video GRPO); OTCA (2604.19234; splits terminal rewards into per-timestep, per-objective credit); "Inference-Time Scaling for Joint Audio-Video Generation" (2606.03183; multi-verifier best-of-N and evolutionary search with Adaptive Reward Weighting); "Refining Multidimensional Video RMs" (2605.28203; disentangled influence functions); DDRL (2512.04332; reward maximisation with forward-KL data regularisation); CoRe (2609.36245; co-evolving RMs against latent reward hacking); Dual-IPO (2502.02088; co-evolving generator and RM). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **Multi-objective RLHF with interpretable dimensions**: VisionReward's MPO aims for "stable and controllable RLHF" balancing multiple preference dimensions; its overall score is a learned linear weighting of checklist answers. — [VisionReward GitHub](https://github.com/zai-org/VisionReward)
- **Over-optimisation controls in flow RL**: Flow-GRPO's GRPO-Guard adds RatioNorm (correcting biased importance-ratio distributions across denoising steps) and gradient reweighting to prevent "excessive optimization under specific noise levels". — [Flow-GRPO GitHub](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo)
- **Ties**: VideoAlign trains with Bradley–Terry-with-Ties (standard BT discards tie labels; BTT reportedly improves robustness/accuracy). — [VideoAlign arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13918v2). SoliReward finds BT on win-lose + win-tie pairs beats BTT in its setting. — [SoliReward arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22170). GenAI-Bench labels ties as two classes (both good / both bad). — [GenAI-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/GenAI-Bench)
- **Uncertainty and distributional rewards**: HPSv3 uses an uncertainty-aware ranking loss modelling each score as N(μ, σ) to absorb annotation subjectivity. — [HPSv3 GitHub](https://github.com/MizzenAI/HPSv3); [Emergent Mind topic](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/human-preference-score-v3-hpsv3). Aligning Human Sense calibrates noisy labels via an Elite Scorer and learns per-dimension reward distributions with Wasserstein alignment, used distributionally inside GRPO (ECCV 2026). — [AHS GitHub](https://github.com/alignhs26/ahs); [arXiv 2608.21425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21425). SURE (2608.06125) uses sample-adaptive uncertainty-aware latent rewards for diffusion post-training. — [SURE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06125)
- **Scale as anti-hacking**: RewardDance finds larger RMs keep reward variance and human-correlation late in RL and reduce hacking. — [RewardDance arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08826v1)
- **Personalised RMs**: UnifiedReward-Flex (Feb–Mar 2026) is a "unified personalized reward model for vision generation" with flexible, context-adaptive reasoning (personalised hierarchical criteria), trained on a 90K SFT set, available on Qwen3-VL and Qwen3.5. — [UnifiedReward GitHub](https://github.com/CodeGoat24/UnifiedReward); [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation). TasteRoute-3k (2610.05896) offers multi-model T2V/I2V comparisons with per-annotator rankings and user profiles; UniPreferBench / UniPrefer (2511.18352) target per-user preference alignment at 100K scale. — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **RL when the generator is a black box (hosted APIs)**: prompt-side optimisation is the trainable lever — VPO (2503.20491; DPO-trained prompt rewriter), Prompt-A-Video (2412.15156; reward-guided prompt evolution), MagicPrompt (2607.14595; soft-prompt tuning with dual-space reward), VQQA (2603.12310; VLM critiques as "semantic gradients" for black-box prompt refinement), "Retrieval, Refinement, and Ranking" (2603.01509; best-of-N over refined prompts with a VQA reward checklist), PhyPrompt (2603.03505; RL prompt refinement for physics). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **Selection value**: extra takes only pay off if the selector's ranking correlates with true quality; CVA audits find selectors often fail to capture sampling headroom once compute is charged. — [arXiv 2609.13257](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13257)

### Inferences
- **Recommended in-app selection stack (per shot, N takes):**
  1. *Gates (non-compensatory):* reject takes failing hard constraints — CGI/3D-look probability above threshold, identity similarity below character threshold, catastrophic anatomy/artifact flags (VBench-2.0 anatomy, VideoReward VQ extreme low). A linear sum must not let a great aesthetic score "buy back" a 3D-render look, which the product goal forbids.
  2. *Per-reward normalisation:* convert each signal to a comparable scale before weighting — z-score against a running per-signal (and ideally per-generator) distribution, shrunk toward global stats because N per shot is small (2–4); GDPO's lesson is to normalise each reward separately rather than normalising the sum.
  3. *Learned aggregation:* fit weights with a Bradley–Terry (or Rao-Kupper/Davidson for ties) model on *within-shot pairs implied by star ratings* (equal stars = tie), features = normalised signals; this calibrates the 7 Claude dimensions *and* the open RMs jointly and is a direct upgrade of the current online star-weighting. Use a hierarchical prior (global weights → per-user/per-project deviations) for personalisation, updated online (Laplace/Thompson).
  4. *Pairwise final:* for the top-2 after aggregation, run a pairwise Claude (both orders) + UnifiedReward-Think comparison as tie-breaker; this avoids pointwise clustering issues.
  5. *Uncertainty → ask the user:* when the top-2 are within the posterior margin or judges disagree (ensemble variance), surface both to the user; those labels are the most informative training data (active learning).
  6. *Monitoring:* track held-out pairwise accuracy of the aggregate vs user choices, per-dimension agreement, and "selection regret" (stars of chosen take vs best available), and watch for hacking by the prompt writer (e.g., over-literal prompts that inflate alignment while lowering look/motion).
- **For model-level RL:** only open-weight models (Wan 2.2; possibly LTX/Hunyuan) can be trained; use DanceGRPO/Flow-GRPO/Pref-GRPO-style multi-reward GRPO with GDPO-style per-reward normalisation, pairwise (win-rate) rewards from a strong judge, KL/data regularisation (DDRL) and GRPO-Guard-style ratio fixes, and hold-out human checks. For hosted models (Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Sora 2), "RL" should target the *Claude planner/prompt-writer* (DPO/RL on prompt pairs whose takes won vs lost) plus selection, using the same reward stack — the literature for black-box prompt optimisation (VPO, VQQA, Retrieval-Refinement-Ranking) supports this.
- Weighted sums remain acceptable *after* gating and normalisation; Pareto filtering (keep takes not dominated on all dimensions, then rank by learned utility) is a simple multi-objective safeguard when weights are uncertain early in a project.

### Gaps
- DanceGRPO's exact multi-reward aggregation (e.g., whether advantages are computed per reward and summed) could not be verified from accessible sources.
- No video-specific evidence was retrieved on classic Pareto/multi-objective diffusion RL methods or weight-averaged/ensemble RMs (e.g., WARM-style), nor on ensemble-disagreement as an uncertainty signal for video RMs; recommendations above are inferred from the LLM/image literature patterns.
- No published study on learning reward weights from *star ratings* (vs pairwise labels) for video selection was found.
- TaRoS, OTCA, CoRe, Adaptive Reward Weighting findings are title-level only.

## Q5. Which benchmarks evaluate video/image reward models and judges, and what are their limitations?

### Takeaway
Sample-level RM benchmarks (VideoGen-RewardBench, GenAI-Bench, MJ-Bench/MJ-Bench-Video, VideoScore-Bench-v2, T2VQA-DB, VideoPhy-2 test) are what matter for take selection, whereas VBench/VBench-2.0/Video-Bench are model-level suites; all are built on 2023–2025 generators and generic prompts, have limited human–human agreement, and some metrics are optimistic (VideoGen-RewardBench's tie-threshold tuning). For a photoreal/surreal story-to-video product, an in-house pairwise benchmark from user ratings is necessary to choose and weight reward models.

### Cited Findings
- **VideoGen-RewardBench (Jan 2025)**: 26.5k video pairs with preference labels, from VideoGen-Eval prompts and modern generators; reports accuracy *without ties* (only A/B-wins pairs) and *with ties* using Algorithm 1 of "Ties Matter", which tries every tie threshold and keeps the best three-class accuracy — hence somewhat optimistic; leaderboard includes independently submitted, unverified entries (search summary of leaderboard). — [VideoGen-RewardBench space](https://kwaivgi-videogen-rewardbench.hf.space); [leaderboard source md.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/KlingTeam/VideoGen-RewardBench/blob/main/src/md.py)
- **GenAI-Bench (arena judge benchmark)**: 1,069 video-generation votes (plus 1,735 image-gen, 919 editing), 4-way labels incl. two tie types; 2024 frontier VLMs ~46–48% on video. — [GenAI-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/GenAI-Bench). Name collision: the *compositional* GenAI-Bench from the VQAScore authors (CVPR 2024 SynData best short paper) is a different, prompt-alignment benchmark. — [t2v_metrics GitHub](https://github.com/linzhiqiu/t2v_metrics)
- **VideoScore-Bench (v1)** ≈7,000 videos (Likert + preference) from VideoFeedback, EvalCrafter, GenAI-Bench, VBench; **VideoScore-Bench-v2** = 500-sample held-out split of VideoFeedback2 (3 dims), where VideoScore2 itself reaches only 44.35% accuracy (search summary). — [VideoScore GitHub](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/VideoScore); [VideoScore2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22799)
- **MJ-Bench (arXiv 2407.04842, Jul 2024)**: tests multimodal judges for T2I across several perspectives (data subsets include alignment and safety); **MJ-Bench-Video / MJ-VIDEO (2502.01719)**: pairwise preferences over 5 aspects and 28 criteria. — [MJ-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/MJ-Bench/MJ-Bench); [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **VBench family**: VBench (Dec 2023; 16 dimensions; VBench++ in TPAMI 2025 adds I2V and trustworthiness), VBench-2.0 (Mar 2025; 18 intrinsic-faithfulness dimensions; customised-video scoring for human anatomy/identity/clothes, diversity, multi-view consistency). — [VBench GitHub](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench); [VBench-2.0 README](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench/blob/HEAD/VBench-2.0/README.md)
- **Video-Bench (Apr 2025)** shows MLLM-based scoring beats VBench's classical metrics on human alignment for every dimension (e.g., 0.733 vs 0.363 imaging; 0.514 vs 0.329 motion) but remains weak on temporal consistency (0.402). — [Video-Bench GitHub](https://github.com/Video-Bench/Video-Bench)
- **Human-label datasets usable as RM test sets**: T2VQA-DB (2403.11956), VideoFeedback (37.6K videos), LGVQ, AIGVQA-DB, LiFT-HRA, CRAVE-DB, Q-EVAL-100K, AIGVE-60K (58.5K videos), VideoFeedback2, GenAI-Arena votes, VGA-BenchV2 (2608.25452; 60K+ videos, 36K annotations, evaluators reused as RL rewards), WorldRewardBench (2605.10434; ~6K expert pairwise comparisons over ~1.4K videos), UVE-Bench (2503.09949; 15 aspects). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **Cinematic / story-relevant suites (title-level)**: FilmBench (2607.24241; film-grade cinematic benchmark, FilmOps judging), Stable Cinemetrics (2509.26555), MSVBench (2602.23969; multi-shot, distilled judge), LoCoT2V-Bench (2510.26412), TC-Bench (2406.08656; scene-transition completion via frame-indexed VLM assertions), T2VTextBench (2505.04946). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation)
- **Known limitations documented by the field**: benchmark label quality (Physics-IQ needed an audit, "Physics-IQ Verified" 2606.18943); social bias propagating from preference data through RMs (VideoBiasEval 2510.17247); verifier bias from execution traces ("Don't Read the Log" 2609.28564); selection gain ≠ benchmark accuracy (2609.13257). — [curated list](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-rm-for-video-generation). Human raters disagree on semantic-consistency dimensions and favour visual appeal (Video-Bench). — [Video-Bench arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04907). Noisy, multi-dimensional human feedback motivates label calibration (Aligning Human Sense). — [AHS GitHub](https://github.com/alignhs26/ahs)
- **Image-RM cross-benchmark generalisation is uneven**: on HPDv3, older RMs fall to 58.6 (ImageReward) – 65.6 (PickScore) and CLIP to 48.6 (below chance), while each RM tends to win on its own home test set (e.g., PickScore 70.5 on Pick-a-Pic, MPS 67.5 on ImageReward-test). — [HPSv3 GitHub](https://github.com/MizzenAI/HPSv3)
- **Absolute physics/semantic success rates of generators remain low** on human-judged physics benchmarks (best 32.6% joint on VideoPhy-2; 21.9% on hard prompts), so physics benchmarks still discriminate between models in 2025-era data. — [VideoPhy-2 README](https://github.com/Hritikbansal/videophy/blob/HEAD/VIDEOPHY2/README.md)

### Inferences
- Public RM benchmarks are a coarse filter for *which* open RMs to trial; they cannot set weights for this app because (a) generators are older than the five hosted models in use, (b) prompts are generic rather than cinematic photoreal/surreal, (c) no benchmark scores "3D-render look" or storyboard-layout adherence, and (d) per-user taste differs.
- Build an in-house "PSC-RewardBench": a few hundred within-shot pairs (with ties) per quarter, stratified by generator, shot type (close-up/wide/action), surreal vs plain, with per-dimension tags when users give them; report pairwise accuracy with and without ties (tie threshold fixed on a dev split, not tuned on test), Kendall τ per dimension, calibration (ECE) of the aggregate, and top-1 *selection regret* vs the user's best take. Re-run whenever a new RM or hosted-model version is added.
- Cross-benchmark image results suggest RMs overfit their own data distributions; expect the same for video RMs — combining several (ensemble) is safer than betting on the single top-of-leaderboard model.

### Gaps
- Full VideoGen-RewardBench leaderboard numbers (per-dimension, with/without ties) and MJ-Bench-Video judge tables could not be accessed (huggingface.co blocked).
- No benchmark covering the specific 2026 hosted models (Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Sora 2) as *sources of candidate pairs* for RM evaluation was found; public arena leaderboards (e.g., Artificial Analysis Video Arena) were not retrieved.
- No benchmark was found for judging photographic vs CGI look, storyboard-layout adherence, or cross-shot character continuity in story-to-video pipelines.
