# Compare, gate and measure every take

Prompt-to-Scene Creator should rebuild its learning loop around one evidence primitive. That primitive is a blind comparison of two takes of the same shot, with two possible ties: "both usable" and "both unusable". The evidence should feed three separately governed learners: a gated, decomposed reward stack that replaces judge weights fitted to star ratings; an in-context optimizer for strategies, exemplars and the director, the only reinforcement lever for Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0 and Sora 2; and on-policy RL for Wan 2.2 alone, anchored to real live-action footage. The 2025–2026 evidence supports this design from three directions: pairwise labels train better video reward models than absolute scores, pairwise prompting raises a VLM judge's agreement with people, and absolute scores fed to group-normalized optimizers create "illusory advantages" that drive reward hacking. The binding constraint is measurement, not algorithms: prompted VLM judges agree with humans on generated video only about 60–73% of the time. At 60% agreement, a strategy that truly wins 60% of human comparisons looks like a 52% winner and needs roughly 4,900 paired comparisons to confirm, against about 305 at 90% agreement. Photorealism must become a gate that no other score can offset, measured by a purpose-built photographic-versus-CGI classifier, because no public reward model measures it and the documented hacks in this space (near-static motion, oversaturated and over-stylized looks, expressionless copy-paste faces) attack the product's core promise. Model-level RL should move from Flow-GRPO with a MixGRPO window to single-step, ratio-normalized or likelihood-free objectives. It should run after supervised fine-tuning and fixed-β DPO and be anchored to real footage, since in NVIDIA's double-blind evaluation a baseline that scored higher on the proxy reward lost human preference. Takes from hosted models are off-policy for Wan, and at least one hosted model's terms reportedly forbid training third-party models on its outputs, so until counsel clears training uses the cross-model preference log is best spent on judge calibration, routing and prompt learning.

## The current loop learns most from its weakest signals

The app's four-timescale design points the right way, and several parts already match 2026 production practice. Its multi-dimensional judge mirrors how frontier labs split rewards. ByteDance's Seedance 1.0 was post-trained against separate foundational, motion and aesthetic reward models ([Seedance 1.0](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09113)). Tencent's HunyuanVideo 1.5 uses MixGRPO, the same sliding-window solver as the app's reference trainer, for its image-to-video online RL ([HunyuanVideo 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18870v1)). The app's prompt learning, model-written strategies selected by Thompson sampling and rewritten by reflection, is in all but name OPTS-style strategy selection combined with GEPA- or ACE-style reflection. Those are the best-supported prompt-learning designs of 2025–2026 ([OPTS](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1070); [GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457)).

The wiring is the problem. The most reliable evidence the product sees is either collapsed into a scalar or never logged: which of two takes of the same shot a user kept, what a "direct this shot" note asked for, and which storyboard panels were approved or edited. The least reliable evidence drives learning. Absolute 1–5 stars enter the strategy posteriors at double weight and are the only target the weight calibrator fits, and the judge emits an absolute score for each take. In LLM studies, preferences derived from ratings and from rankings **disagreed about 60% of the time** ([Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.15812v3)). On image tasks, a GPT-4V judge reached about **79% agreement in pairwise mode but only a Pearson correlation of 0.49 when scoring absolutely** ([MLLM-as-a-Judge](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.04788)).

Three structural problems make this worse. First, the calibrator can trade away the product's core constraint. One user's stars can push the photorealism weight down to its 0.02 floor, although "never 3D animation" is a product requirement rather than a matter of taste. Every online weight change also silently redefines the reward that L1 and L3 optimize, so both chase a target they are helping to move. Second, each context learns alone. Every target × style × kind context has its own pool, yet a simple power calculation shows that telling a 60%-win strategy from a 50%-win one takes **about 194 paired comparisons**. That is more than most contexts will see before a vendor update makes the evidence stale. Third, the logs cannot support later correction. The judge sees the prompt Claude wrote, which invites self-preference and priming by the prompt's wording. `preferences.jsonl` omits exposure, screen position, judge and model versions, vendor-rewriter state, seeds and sampling propensities, so position bias, drift and off-policy evaluation cannot be handled after the fact. The table maps each layer to the change the evidence supports, and the sections that follow give the reasoning.

|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit feedback | 1–5 stars, comment, A/B, "direct this shot" | Blind within-shot A/B with four outcomes; one-tap reason chips per judge dimension; segment and region flags; stars optional and converted into within-user comparisons | Pick-a-Pic, VideoAlign, GenAI-Bench, DenseDPO, RichHF |
| Implicit feedback | Not used as preference data | Pairs within a matched context from approve, timeline, export, re-roll and delete, with exposure logged | RLUF, Pick-a-Pic loop |
| Judge | Claude vision, 7 absolute scores per take, sees the prompt | Pairwise in both orders; checklist before score; motion evidence; blind to prompt and provider; a non-Claude ensemble member; specialist signals | GenArena, VideoScore2, VR-Thinker, Video-Bench |

## Within-shot comparisons with two kinds of tie should replace stars

### Paired takes with a tie option outperform stars and grids

Comparing two outputs of the same prompt is the best-supported primary feedback format for generative media. Pick-a-Pic tested three interfaces and found that **two images with a tie option beat both four images and pairs without ties on engagement and inter-rater agreement**. Its loop also made labeling the product action itself: the rejected image is replaced and the user keeps working ([Pick-a-Pic](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2305.01569); [Stability AI](https://stability.ai/research/pick-a-pic)). For video, a Bradley–Terry reward model trained on pairs **consistently outperformed a pointwise regression model** across dataset sizes, though the gap narrowed as data grew ([VideoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13918v2)). Switching a Qwen3-VL-8B judge from pointwise to pairwise scoring raised its agreement with human choices on VideoGen-RewardBench **from 57.0% to 61.5%** ([GenArena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06013)). The tie should come in two kinds, as in GenAI-Arena's four-way labels (A better, B better, both good, both bad) ([GenAI-Bench](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/GenAI-Bench)). A "both unusable" tie is a negative label on two takes, while a "both usable" tie says the difference does not matter.

Expectations should be calibrated accordingly. PickScore predicts real users' Pick-a-Pic choices with 70.2% accuracy, against 68.0% for expert humans ([NeurIPS 2023](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72229)), so 65–75% agreement with one person's pairwise picks is near the ceiling, not a failure. Stars can stay as an optional, low-weight signal, used only as relative comparisons within one user, session and shot. When Netflix replaced five stars with thumbs, tests with hundreds of thousands of members produced **200% more ratings** ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/2017/04/05/netflix-ditches-5-star-rating-system-for-thumbs-up-and-thumbs-down)). When a shot has three or more takes, asking for the best (and optionally the worst) yields pairs without forcing a full ranking.

### Richer formats explain why and where a take failed

A single overall preference cannot say why a take won or where it failed. The 2024–2026 datasets add those layers on top of the pairwise core rather than replacing it. VisionReward splits video preference into **64 yes/no checklist questions** combined with learned linear weights, and reports 17.2% better preference prediction than VideoScore ([VisionReward](https://huggingface.co/papers/2412.21059)). FIRM-Video checks each criterion against evidence from specific moments in the clip before it scores ([FIRM-Video](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21839)). Multi-dimensional preference data shows that people's choices change with the aspect being judged ([MPS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Zhang_Learning_Multi-Dimensional_Human_Preference_for_Text-to-Image_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.html)). The app's version is a set of one-tap reason chips, one per judge dimension, attached to every A/B choice or thumb.

Localization is cheap and valuable. RichHF turns clicked points into heatmap disks with a radius of 1/20 of the image height and uses them to build inpainting masks ([RichHF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10240)). DenseDPO labels **segments of roughly one second**; with one third of the labels it matched vanilla DPO on alignment and visual quality while improving motion ([DenseDPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03517)). "Flag a moment" and "tap the problem area" should therefore store `(take, t_start, t_end, x, y, radius, dimension)`.

Free text is the richest signal. From every "direct this shot" note, Claude should extract structured tuples: dimension, polarity, region or time, attribute and desired change. When the revision is approved, the app should record `revised ≻ original` together with the stated reason. It should also keep CIPHER-style natural-language preference statements. In Microsoft's simulated-user experiments these had the lowest edit cost of the methods tested, and the learned preferences stayed readable and editable ([CIPHER](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/aligning-llm-agents-by-learning-latent-preference-from-user-edits/)).

### Implicit signals supply volume but need matched contexts

Implicit behavior supplies the volume. No vendor documents how re-rolls, upscales or downloads become labels, so the app must define its own taxonomy and validate it against explicit A/B labels. Pairs should form only within a matched context (same project, shot, storyboard frame and prompt version) and only between takes the user actually saw. A take the user never saw has not been rejected.

Two failure modes are documented. Meta optimized a reward model for sparse "Love" reactions, which occur on about 0.1% of messages, and raised them **by up to 28% in live tests**. The same optimization produced hacked "Bye! Sending Love!" sign-offs ([RLUF](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14946v1)), so no single engagement proxy should become the objective. Annotators comparing independently generated videos also **favor low-motion clips** because those show fewer artifacts ([DenseDPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03517)). Motion therefore needs its own label or motion-matched pairs; otherwise user feedback itself will teach the system to make static shots.

| Signal | Capture | Becomes | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Within-shot A/B (A, B, both usable, both unusable) | Sides randomized; side and exposure logged | Pairs per dimension, with two tie types | Primary label for judge calibration, bandit duels and, where consent and terms allow, reward-model data |
| Reason chips (7 dimensions) | One tap on any choice | Yes/no checklist labels per dimension | Calibrating the judge's checklist; dimension-specific rewards |
| Flag a moment / tap an area | `(take, t0, t1, x, y, r, dim)` | Segment and region labels | Segment-level DPO pairs, judge evidence, regional regeneration |
| "Direct this shot", plan and panel edits | Raw text plus tuples extracted by Claude | `revised ≻ original` pairs, preference statements | Director and prompt-writer memory |
| Approve, place in timeline, export | Event log | Approved take ≻ each alternative the user viewed | Strong positive |
| Extend, use as reference, variations | Event log | Moderately positive pairs | Evidence for routing and strategies |
| Re-roll without selecting, delete, revert | Event log, viewed takes only | Weak or full negatives | Down-weighted until validated |
| Stars | Optional | Comparisons within one user and one shot | Low weight; never used as absolute scores |

### Ask sparingly and aggregate with Bradley–Terry, never Elo

Organic feedback is sparse, so the app should ask for labels, but only when the answer is informative. That means three cases: the judge's top two takes fall within its uncertainty margin, the judge disagrees with what the user actually did, or a routing or template decision lacks enough data. Asks should be capped per session and never interrupt playback. Choosing queries by optimal experimental design personalized a text-to-image model with fewer queries than random selection ([ED-PBRL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19057)). How often end users of creative tools will tolerate such questions is undocumented, so the cap itself should be tuned as an experiment.

Aggregation should use a Bradley–Terry model that handles ties (the Rao–Kupper or Davidson variants) for each dimension. The model should include a random effect, a left/right bias term and a reliability estimate for each user, in the Crowd-BT tradition ([Crowd-BT](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=165700)), and report bootstrap intervals. A running Elo rating should never drive decisions. Chatbot Arena moved to Bradley–Terry because **online Elo shifted substantially when the same matches were replayed in reverse order** ([LMSYS](https://lmsys.org/blog/2023-12-07-leaderboard)).

Global decisions, such as Veo 3.1 versus Kling 3.0 for handheld night exteriors, need data pooled across users. A 2026 power analysis found that preference margins of 0.10 or less need **at least 500 judgments for 90% power**, that sample size grows with the inverse square of the margin, and that a 30–100-judgment pilot should come first ([Lee 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09084v1)). Three checks measure each user's consistency: randomizing which side each take appears on, re-showing an occasional pair with sides swapped, and seeding obvious "sanity" pairs. The same swap test applied to the judge turns disagreement between the two orders into an uncertainty estimate.

### A trained-rater gold program anchors everything else

In-product feedback needs a counterpart from trained raters. This report proposes a gold program of **300–1,000 shot-level pairs**, spanning the five targets and the product's hard cases: photoreal close-ups of faces and hands, crowds, low light, continuity across several characters, and photographic-surreal integration. At least three trained raters should label each pair per dimension, and the full vote vector should be kept so that training can account for uncertainty. That follows the recipe of the large preference datasets ([HPSv3](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03789v1); [VideoFeedback2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22799v1)). HPDv3 deliberately mixed **real photographs of varying quality** into its comparisons, and the app should likewise include licensed live-action footage to anchor the photographic end of the scale.

The rater guide should be written as yes/no checks, such as "Does the skin read as plastic?", "Does the light fall off like a render?", "Does the identity match the reference sheet?" and "Does the camera move match the storyboard?". Raters should point to the time and region behind each "no". Hidden gold pairs and swapped repeats then weight each rater, as Rapidata does with its per-rater userScore ([Rapidata](https://gitcode.com/hf_mirrors/Rapidata/text-2-video-human-preferences-veo3.1/blob/main/README.md)). Model-labeled pairs are a cheap supplement, not a substitute. By the authors' estimate, a reward-model-labeled set comparable to Pick-a-Pic v2 cost about **$200, against about $50,000** for the human version ([RankDPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18013)).

### Global gates plus per-project taste handle diverse users

Taste diversity calls for two tiers. Product standards (photographic live-action look, artifact-free motion, continuity and fidelity to the shot) are learned from everyone and act as gates. Taste dimensions (grade, lens habits, pacing, motion energy, degree of surrealism and film-stock look) are re-weighted per user and per project.

The first personal layer should be a per-project Style Memory. It holds short preference statements that Claude extracts from critiques, edits and A/B reasons, each carrying its source events and a confidence. The statements are injected into planning and prompt writing, and the user can edit them. In ViPer's user studies, images steered by comment-derived preferences were chosen over unpersonalized ones **86.1%** of the time (secondary figure) ([ViPer](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/9492_ECCV_2024_paper.php)).

Learned user embeddings come later, for re-ranking takes and routing rather than for conditioning hosted models. An embedding of **as few as four** of a new user's pairwise choices reached a 76% win rate over the base model, as judged by a VLM ([PPD](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Dang_Personalized_Preference_Fine-tuning_of_Diffusion_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)). Latent-mixture reward models generalize to unseen users from few samples ([PAL](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/2858f8c8683aaa8c12d487354cf328dc-Abstract-Conference.html)). An optional calibration of 10–20 well-chosen pairs per project genre is therefore a reasonable start. That is far below the roughly 200 ratings Midjourney reportedly required before V7 personalization ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/tutorial/midjourney-v7)). No personalization study for video was found. A user's own feedback should dominate their personalization, while each account's weight in the global model is capped.

### Storage and consent decide what the data can be used for

Feedback belongs in an append-only event log keyed by pseudonymous user and project IDs. Each event carries a full snapshot of its context: prompt and strategy versions, storyboard frame, provider, model version, rewriter state, seed, judge version and scores, screen position, exposure time and sampling propensity. Every derived table is rebuilt from the log, so deletion and opt-out propagate by recomputation.

Consent needs two plainly worded toggles, one to personalize the user's own results and one to improve shared models, and each feedback widget should state which it implies. The cautionary precedents are concrete. In ChatGPT, a thumbs rating can cause "the entire conversation" to be used for training even after an opt-out ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/how-your-data-is-used-to-improve-model-performance/)). Google Flow keeps human-reviewed data separately even after the user deletes it ([Google Flow privacy](https://labs.google/flow/privacy)). In the EU, legitimate interest can justify model training only after a documented three-step test ([EDPB Opinion 28/2024](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/edpb-opinion-28-2024-key-takeaways-on-processing-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models)), so consent-based defaults are the safer starting point. Stories and critiques sent to Claude through the commercial API are not used to train Anthropic's models ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)).

## Prompted video judges match humans only 60–73% of the time

### Judges are weakest where video differs from images

Prompted vision-language judges are mediocre on generated video, and weakest where video differs from images. On GenAI-Arena's 1,069 human video votes, GPT-4o reached **48.46% four-way accuracy** and Gemini-1.5-Pro 46.21%, against a random baseline of 25.16%. Claude was listed only as a to-do ([GenAI-Bench](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/GenAI-Bench)). An MLLM evaluator correlated with humans at 0.70–0.75 on static attributes such as imaging quality, color and object class, but only **0.402 on temporal consistency and 0.514 on motion** ([Video-Bench](https://github.com/Video-Bench/Video-Bench)).

Newer judges do better without closing the gap. On VGI-Bench (August 2026), Gemini-3-Flash reached 73.2% pairwise accuracy, GPT-5-mini 64.3% and Claude Haiku 4.5 47.9%, though the protocol and chance level were not confirmed ([VGI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19583)). The MEDit-Bench judge agreed with humans **59.9% of the time (Cohen's κ = 0.18)** and favored whichever video was shown first ([MEDit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25300)). The Haiku figure is the only published Claude number found. It says nothing about Opus-class judging, but it means the app's judge stays unvalidated until it is measured against the app's own labels.

Judges also err in characteristic directions. They favor linguistic plausibility over visual correctness ([Perceptual Judgment Bias](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02578v1)). They over-penalize brief frame-level artifacts that people barely notice ([Co-Director](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24842)). They also favor their own generations ([LLM self-preference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)), which matters here because Claude both writes the prompts and judges the takes. Trained reward models that reason before scoring do markedly better. VR-Thinker's 7B model, which requests extra frames when unsure, reports **80.5% on VideoGen-RewardBench** (third-party figure) ([VR-Thinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10518v1)). The Claude judge should therefore be trusted most on static dimensions visible in single frames, and co-scored by specialist models on motion and identity drift.

### Agreement sets the experimentation budget

The case for investing in agreement is arithmetic. If a judge agrees with humans with probability *a*, the observed win rate of a change that people prefer with probability *p* is 0.5 + (2*a* − 1)(*p* − 0.5). The table shows how many comparisons a standard two-sided test (α = 0.05, 80% power) needs to detect a true 60% human win rate.

| Who labels the comparisons | Observed win rate | Comparisons needed |
|---|---|---|
| Humans | 60% | about 194 |
| Judge with 60% agreement | 52% | about 4,900 |
| Judge with 80% agreement | 56% | about 543 |
| Judge with 90% agreement | 58% | about 305 |

Every point of judge agreement buys back a large share of the experimentation budget, so improving the judge deserves its own owner and budget. One self-reported case shows how much is available: optimizing an LLM judge's prompt with GEPA raised its accuracy from 68.88% to 88.89% ([GEPA](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa)). Choosing among takes follows the same logic. A 2026 audit of test-time scaling for video world models found that bigger candidate pools raise the best quality available, but **selectors fail to capture that headroom** once costs are counted ([Sampling headroom is not selection gain](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13257)). An analogous LLM study found that the share captured tracks the verifier's correlation with true quality ([LLM analogue](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18931)).

### Five changes to the judge protocol

The judge protocol should change in five ways. First, it should compare takes of the same shot pairwise, in both orders and with the four-way tie, instead of scoring each take on an absolute scale. Second, it should check before it scores, answering a per-dimension list of yes/no questions with cited timestamps before giving any verdict. Reasoning first pays off: removing chain-of-thought dropped VideoScore2's in-domain accuracy **from 39.81 to 32.17** ([VideoScore2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22799)). The evaluation owner should fix and version product-level checks such as photographic look, render-like lighting and plastic skin. Claude can write shot-level checks from the shot spec before any pixels exist. That keeps the rubric model-driven without letting it be tailored to a particular output.

Third, it should see temporal evidence: at least 2 frames per second with timestamps, a frame-difference or optical-flow strip, and a second round in which it can request specific frames. Fourth, it should judge blind. It should not see the provider prompt, the strategy or the model identity, and should measure fidelity to the pre-generation shot spec and blueprint, which is what the user cares about. Fifth, its output should be read as probabilities rather than integers, sampled several times, and combined in an ensemble with at least one non-Claude judge, such as UnifiedReward-Think, so that errors are less correlated.

### No off-the-shelf model scores the photographic look

No off-the-shelf reward model scores the property the product exists to deliver. AI-generated-video detectors such as DeMamba answer whether a clip is generated, not whether it looks photographic ([DeMamba](https://github.com/chenhaoxing/DeMamba)). Every hosted output is generated, so such detectors would saturate and track generator fingerprints rather than look.

The highest-value custom component is therefore a lightweight probe on frozen DINOv2 or SigLIP features from three to eight frames per take. Its positives are real film and photo stills plus accepted live-action takes. Its negatives are frames from 3D renders, game engines and anime, frames with an "AI-plastic" look, and takes users flagged as rejects. Because both classes contain generated frames, the probe has to learn look rather than provenance.

A second opinion can come from a contrastive yes-probability probe. It scores a frame on "a frame from live-action footage shot on a real camera" minus "a frame from a 3D render". This combines VQAScore-style P("yes") scoring ([t2v_metrics](https://github.com/linzhiqiu/t2v_metrics)) with SRPO's trick of differencing rewards between positive and negative control words to cancel reward bias ([SRPO](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2509.06942)). HPSv3, whose training data includes real photographs, adds a photographic prior on keyframes ([HPSv3](https://github.com/MizzenAI/HPSv3)).

### Workable specialists exist for the other dimensions

Identity can be scored with face and person-re-identification embeddings against both the character sheet and the last accepted shot. Reporting minimum and median similarity across frames catches drift within a clip. Motion should combine VideoReward's motion head with optical-flow magnitude, judged against the motion the shot plan calls for. Classical temporal metrics correlate with people at only **0.26–0.33** ([Video-Bench](https://github.com/Video-Bench/Video-Bench)), so their weights should be learned from labels rather than set by hand.

Composition is the app's unusual advantage. An open-vocabulary detector runs on keyframes and its detections are matched to the blueprint's boxes. That gives a deterministic, verifiable reward (box overlap, centroid offset and scale error) of the kind T2V-CompBench uses for spatial relationships ([T2V-CompBench](https://github.com/KaiyueSun98/T2V-CompBench)). No published work has yet validated it against hosted models. Surreal integration needs care, because physics evaluators will score intended impossibilities as violations. Planned violations must be masked or declared. Integration should instead be scored as physically consistent rendering of the impossible element: lighting, shadow, contact and scale.

| Dimension | Primary signal | Second opinion | Role | Known trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Photographic look (anti-CGI) | Custom probe on frozen features | Contrastive P("yes") probe; HPSv3 on keyframes | Gate plus feature | Glossy "clean CG" scores as quality |
| Fidelity to the shot spec | VideoReward text head; VQAScore with a Qwen3-VL backend | Claude checklist | Feature | Over-literal prompts; unstable as an RL reward |
| Composition vs storyboard | Detector boxes vs blueprint (IoU, offset, scale) | Claude | Feature | Rewards literal, centered framing |
| Character continuity | Face and re-ID embeddings vs character sheet and last shot | Claude for hair, costume, age | Gate per character, plus feature | Expressionless copy-paste faces |
| Motion | VideoReward motion head; flow magnitude vs plan; flicker | Claude with motion strips | Feature plus a "too static" floor | Static bias in users, reward models and judges |
| Surreal integration | Claude rubric on light, shadow, contact, scale | Physics evaluator with planned violations masked | Feature | Physics models penalize intent |
| Aesthetic | HPSv3, Q-Align | Claude | Taste feature with per-user weight | Aesthetic-only rewards push static, stylized output |
| Safety and likeness | Provider moderation, own classifiers, consent records | — | Hard gate outside the reward | Learning to evade moderation |

The strongest shortlist of open models to trial has four entries. VideoReward is a 2B-parameter, MIT-licensed model with heads for visual quality, motion and text alignment ([VideoAlign](https://github.com/KwaiVGI/VideoAlign)). VideoScore2 adds a physics axis and written rationales. UnifiedReward-2.0 and -Think on Qwen3-VL or Qwen3.5 compare videos pairwise natively ([UnifiedReward](https://github.com/CodeGoat24/UnifiedReward)). VideoPhy-2's auto-evaluator judges physics rule by rule ([VideoPhy-2](https://github.com/Hritikbansal/videophy/blob/HEAD/VIDEOPHY2/README.md)). All were trained on 2024–2025 generators, and how well they separate Seedance 2.0 or Veo 3.1 takes is untested. Only VideoAlign's MIT license was confirmed.

### Signals combine in a fixed order

The signals combine in a fixed order. Gates come first and cannot be offset, so a strong aesthetic score can never buy back a render look, a broken identity or a catastrophic artifact. Each surviving signal is then normalized separately against a running distribution for that signal, ideally per generator, shrunk toward global statistics because a shot has only two to four takes. NVIDIA's GDPO showed in LLM experiments that summing rewards before group normalization **collapses distinct reward combinations into identical advantages** ([GDPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05242v1)).

Weights are learned next. A Bradley–Terry model with ties, fitted on within-shot pairs, replaces the current star regression. Its features are the normalized Claude dimensions and open-model scores, with a hierarchical prior from global weights down to per-user and per-project deviations. The top two takes then go to a pairwise final in both orders, with a judge from a second model family breaking ties. If they remain within the posterior margin, or the judges disagree, the app asks the user, which yields the most informative labels the product can collect.

Every weight set is a versioned reward function. While weights are still uncertain early in a project, Pareto filtering is a simple safeguard: keep only takes that no other take beats on every dimension, then rank them by learned utility.

### Public benchmarks shortlist models but cannot weight them

Public benchmarks can shortlist reward models but cannot weight them for this product. They use older generators and generic prompts, and none scores render look or storyboard adherence. Reward models also overfit their home data: CLIP falls to **48.6%, below chance**, on HPDv3, while each model tends to win on its own test set ([HPSv3](https://github.com/MizzenAI/HPSv3)). VideoGen-RewardBench even picks its best tie threshold on the evaluation data, which flatters results ([VideoGen-RewardBench](https://kwaivgi-videogen-rewardbench.hf.space)).

The app needs its own benchmark, built from the gold program and refreshed quarterly. It should hold within-shot pairs stratified by generator, shot type and surreal versus plain. It should report pairwise accuracy with and without ties (with the tie threshold fixed on a development split), Kendall τ per dimension, calibration error, and top-1 selection regret against the user's best take. It should be rerun whenever a reward model or a hosted model version changes.

## Hosted models learn through prompts, and only Wan 2.2 through weights

Four of the five targets are closed. For Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0 and Sora 2, "reinforcement learning" therefore means optimizing three things: the planning and prompting policy, the routing of shots to providers, and the selection of takes. The black-box prompt-optimization literature supports this split ([VQQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12310)). Wan 2.2's Apache-2.0 weights make it the only target for weight-level RL. The prompt layer is also where the app's existing machinery is closest to the state of the art and cheapest to improve.

### Strategy learning needs pooled priors, duels and a control arm

Thompson sampling over model-written strategies has direct support. In OPTS, Thompson-sampling selection beat both uniform and LLM-chosen selection and improved EvoPrompt by **up to 50%**. Its design also includes an "inaction" arm, which the app lacks ([OPTS](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1070)).

The upgrades are statistical. Contexts should share evidence through hierarchical priors. Each target × style × mode cell starts from its parent's posterior (global, then target, then target × style), capped at five to ten pseudo-observations, as hierarchical Bayesian bandits share strength across tasks ([HierTS](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/hong22c.html)). When reflection proposes a new strategy, Claude's own forecast of its success can become a prior worth two to four pseudo-counts, centered on the parent. This follows LLM-warm-started Bayesian optimization ([LLAMBO](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/84b8d9fcb4e262fcd429544697e1e720-Abstract-Conference.html)) and keeps the system model-driven while letting data quickly override the prior. A frozen control strategy in every context makes "worse than baseline" detectable and exposes vendor drift.

Rewards should be calibrated probabilities from the reward stack. Ratings should enter only as outcomes relative to the same user's other ratings, weighted by that rater's reliability. Whenever takes from two strategies exist for one shot, the update should be a duel. One option is Double Thompson sampling aimed at the strategy that wins the most pairwise contests, a Copeland winner ([PDO](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13907v2)). Another is neural dueling selection driven by human preferences alone ([APOHF](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17346v1)).

Selection should also carry a cost term: sample θ and choose the arm that maximizes θ − λ·cost, where cost is price per second times duration plus expected regenerations. This follows budget-aware routing such as PILOT ([PILOT](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1301)). Choosing the provider suits a contextual bandit over shot-description embeddings, which learns that different generators win on different kinds of shot ([PAK-UCB](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13287v2)).

### The bandit must track vendor changes

The bandit is non-stationary twice over: reflection keeps adding arms, and vendors silently change their models and rewriters. On Vertex AI, Veo's Gemini-based `enhancePrompt` rewriter is **on by default** and returns its rewritten prompt only when the original is under 30 words. Whether that default holds for Veo 3.1 is unconfirmed ([Vertex AI](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/turn-the-prompt-rewriter-off)). HunyuanVideo 1.5 and Wan 2.2 both ship or recommend LLM rewriting ([HunyuanVideo 1.5](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-1.5); [Wan 2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)). The app is therefore optimizing a pre-prompt that the vendor may rewrite.

The bandit should respond in four ways. It should key contexts by model version, with each new version inheriting the previous version's posterior at reduced weight. It should discount old counts, for example by 0.98–0.995 per day (an unsourced heuristic). It should re-explore when the control arm's win rate shifts. And it should log the rewriter's state, treating it as an arm wherever a toggle exists.

Rewriters also restyle content. HunyuanVideo 1.5's published examples turn realistic prompts into "cinematic photography realistic style" but turn an ink-painting prompt into an animation style. Final prompts should therefore state photographic live-action intent explicitly, especially for surreal content.

Each selection should also log its propensity at decision time: the Monte Carlo probability that the chosen arm was the posterior's top choice. Logged propensities enable inverse-propensity and doubly robust replay ([Dudík et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1103.4601)), as well as estimators built for free-form prompts ([Kiyohara et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02646v1)). Rewritten strategies can then be screened offline before they cost generations.

### Reflection should be evidence-driven and conservative

GEPA mutates prompts through natural-language reflection on rollouts and keeps a Pareto front of candidates. It beat GRPO by 6 points on average with **up to 35× fewer rollouts**, and beat MIPROv2 by more than 10 points ([GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457)). ACE identified two failure modes of rewriting whole prompts. "Brevity bias" makes prompts drift toward short, generic text, and "context collapse" erodes accumulated detail. ACE fixed both with itemized small edits that carry helpful and harmful counters ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v1)).

The app's reflection call should therefore change in four ways. It should receive per-dimension critiques, cited frames and failure traces rather than averages. It should edit strategies item by item rather than rewriting them whole. It should keep any strategy that is best for some sub-context or objective. And it should leave retirement to statistics: a strategy is retired only when it is worse than the control arm with high posterior probability and sits on no Pareto front. Reflection on its own is not trustworthy, because LLM optimizers produce similar feedback whatever the error distribution and lean on their own priors ([Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02101v1)).

Budgets must be translated into video cost. DSPy's formula puts a "light" GEPA run on a one-component program at about **420–580 metric calls** for validation sets of 10–50 items ([DSPy GEPA](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/blob/main/dspy/teleprompt/gepa/gepa.py)). That is impossible per context when each call is a judged video. Runs should be capped at roughly 60–150 calls over 8–12 validation scenes. Candidates should first be screened on the keyframes the app already renders, with video spent only on finalists, as multi-fidelity prompt search does ([HbBoPs](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/schneider25b.html)). Promotion should use sequential halving ([TRIPLE](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b46bc1449205888e1883f692aff1a252-Abstract.html)).

### Test-time critique and long-term memory should run as coupled loops

VISTA, a Google agent built around Veo 3, plans a video, generates candidates, runs a pairwise tournament judged by a multimodal LLM, then critiques and rewrites the prompt. It reached **up to a 60% pairwise win rate** against strong baselines, and human raters preferred it in 66.4–68% of comparisons ([VISTA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Long_VISTA_A_Test-Time_Self-Improving_Video_Generation_Agent_CVPR_2026_paper.html)).

The app should reserve this inner loop, capped at two or three rounds, for hero shots and shots that failed checks. Its tournaments should feed the long-term loop as contrastive evidence: the same scene, different prompts, which won and why. This is how ReasoningBank's memory-aware test-time scaling compounds its gains ([ReasoningBank](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/980ea04d23d1f6908964eba2a74afe45-Abstract-Conference.html)).

Exemplar memory needs four changes. It should retrieve by scene similarity plus a diversity term rather than by top score alone. It should tag every exemplar with its model version and the judge's rationale. It should store winning and losing prompts for the same scene together. And it should treat exemplar sets as arms in their own right.

### The director learns from plan edits, not fine-tuning

Weight-level learning for Claude is effectively unavailable. A late-2026 secondary report found **no documented fine-tuning route for Claude Sonnet or Opus** as of 30 October 2026, though earlier sources conflict ([Ertas](https://www.ertas.ai/blog/can-you-fine-tune-claude)). Secondary coverage also reports that OpenAI's self-serve fine-tuning has closed to new users ([Tessl](https://tessl.io/blog/openai-shutting-fine-tuning-signals-for-enterprise-ai)).

The director should therefore learn in context, and it receives the cheapest feedback in the product. Storyboard approvals, panel edits and accepted-versus-proposed shot lists all arrive before any video is paid for. Every plan, layout and revision should be logged with the accepted version and a structured diff. That yields DPO-format pairs and an edit-cost signal. Three mechanisms turn the log into learning without weight updates: CIPHER-style preference statements retrieved by story or scene similarity, an ACE-style director playbook with helpful and harmful counters, and GEPA's module-targeted reflection over the planning instructions.

Credit must be split, because a weak take can be the director's fault (the wrong shot), the prompt writer's (the wrong words) or the provider's. Plan edits and approvals should train the director. Per-shot outcomes should train the prompt writer. Routing outcomes should train the provider bandit.

SPIRAL contrasts valid plans with **deliberately flawed alternatives**, such as continuity breaks, impossible blocking and off-style 3D looks ([SPIRAL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08403)). The idea works without training: Claude can generate such variants as contrastive examples and as calibration items for the judge.

If weight learning becomes necessary, the feasible configuration is a hybrid. Claude stays the director, and a small open-weight prompt rewriter for each target is distilled from logged preferences. It would be trained with supervised fine-tuning, then DPO or GRPO, through a managed LoRA service ([Tinker](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai)). This follows VPO and Prompt-A-Video ([VPO](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.20491); [Prompt-A-Video](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Ji_Prompt-A-Video_Prompt_Your_Video_Diffusion_Model_via_Preference-Aligned_LLM_ICCV_2025_paper.html)).

AI labels can stand in for human ones on text-level plan quality. On summarization, RLAIF and RLHF beat the supervised baseline in 71% and 73% of human comparisons, a difference that was not significant ([RLAIF](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.00267v3)). No comparable evidence exists for video-level judgments. Any learned rewriter must clear a non-inferiority check on every objective. Promptist's aesthetics-optimized rewriter **reduced text–image consistency by about 3.3–3.6%**, while OPT2I's consistency-driven loop raised it by up to 24.9% ([OPT2I](https://huggingface.co/papers/2403.17804)).

### Wan 2.2 should train on its own rollouts, not the cross-model log

The preference log is the wrong training set for Wan. Takes from Seedance, Veo, Kling and Sora are off-policy for Wan. Off-policy DPO on video "failed to yield significant performance improvements and even led to notable degradation" in dynamic degree ([OnlineVPO](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/papers/Zhang_Align_Video_Diffusion_Model_with_Online_Video-Centric_Preference_Optimization_WACV_2026_paper.pdf)). StepFun, by contrast, found that pairs drawn from multiple seeds of the model's own outputs **kept updates smooth and training stable** ([Step-Video-T2V](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10248v3)).

The log is also exposed contractually. Third-party trackers report that Kling's terms bar using its outputs to train third-party AI models ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/kling-ai/kling-ai-terms-of-service/)). Two other vendors, Runway and Luma, forbid training competing models on their outputs ([Runway](https://conductatlas.com/platform/runway/runway-usage-policy/prohibition-on-using-outputs-to-train-competing-ai-models/); [Luma](https://conductatlas.com/platform/luma-ai/luma-ai-terms-of-service/prohibition-on-using-output-to-train-competing-ai-models/)). The API terms for Seedance, Veo, Sora 2 and hosted Wan were not reviewed. The log should therefore calibrate judges, routing and prompts, and counsel should clear any use of it to train a reward model or generator. Wan itself should learn from its own rollouts and from licensed real footage.

### Fixed-β DPO and a real-footage anchor come before online RL

The industrial reports converge on a stage order: supervised fine-tuning, then offline preference optimization, then online RL. HunyuanVideo 1.5 runs offline DPO first precisely because existing reward models **struggle to distinguish fine-grained motion quality** ([HunyuanVideo 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18870v1)). For the app, SFT on licensed live-action footage sets the look prior.

The DPO stage should use a fixed β. A β that varied with the timestep made Flow-DPO perform poorly, while a fixed β made it the most effective method ([VideoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13918v2)). It should train on three kinds of pairs. The first is human-labeled pairs of Wan's own takes. The second is DenseDPO-style pairs that share the same underlying motion, labeled per segment; a frontier VLM labels these nearly as well as people. The third is real-versus-generated pairs in the style of RealDPO, where the real clip wins, which aim directly at the render look ([RealDPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14955v2)).

The anchor matters more than the algorithm. NVIDIA's DDRL replaces on-policy KL with a forward-KL term to real data, and names on-policy regularization "the primary cause of reward hacking". In its Cosmos comparison, DanceGRPO reached a higher VideoAlign reward (0.715 against 0.604) but **lost 10.5% human preference, while DDRL held level**. The evaluation drew on about 10,000 double-blind votes, though the per-method figures come from a secondary summary ([DDRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04332); [EmergentMind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2512.04332)). A curated live-action corpus used as that anchor also pulls the model away from the CG look.

### Single-step and likelihood-free objectives should replace the current trainer

Flow-GRPO with a MixGRPO window is a production-proven recipe. MixGRPO cut training time by **about 50% versus DanceGRPO**, and by 71% with a higher-order solver outside the window ([MixGRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21802v3)). The 2026 video work points elsewhere, in four directions.

Single-step training is the first. Flash-GRPO trains one stochastic timestep shared by all members of a group ("iso-temporal grouping"), with temporal gradient rectification. Without rectification, **reward collapsed catastrophically between 300 and 400 GPU-hours** ([Flash-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15980)). LingBot-Video adopted one stochastic step drawn from the first half of the schedule. It argues that sharing one trajectory-level advantage across many stochastic steps creates "a severe credit-assignment problem" ([LingBot-Video](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07675)).

Ratio normalization is the second. GRPO-Guard found that the importance ratio's mean sits consistently below one at low-noise steps, which defeats clipping. Normalizing the ratio raised the gold GenEval score to **0.89 against 0.84** for Flow-GRPO ([GRPO-Guard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.22319)).

Likelihood-free objectives are the third, and they avoid per-step log-probabilities entirely. DiffusionNFT reports 3–25× better efficiency, works with any solver and runs without classifier-free guidance ([DiffusionNFT](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/d8e68ddfe22520b45b8fb8d5cbde5a21-Abstract-Conference.html)). DGPO reports about a 20× training speedup over Flow-GRPO ([DGPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08425)). DiffusionART matched the late-stage reward of DiffusionNFT and Flow-GRPO-Fast on HunyuanVideo-13B with **about 12× fewer GPU-hours**. It accepts pairwise reward models and needs no differentiable one ([DiffusionART](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/2610.12572)).

Reward handling is the fourth. The app's advantage formula already normalizes each reward within its group. It should add GDPO's batch-wise renormalization, a floor on the standard deviation and pairwise win-rate rewards. Group-normalizing tightly clustered pointwise scores inflates trivial gaps into **"illusory advantages"**, which drove one model toward an "unnaturally dark style" ([Pref-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20751v1)).

Wan's mixture of experts adds a twist. In the Miles recipe, timesteps at or above 0.875 update only the high-noise expert, which lays out composition and coarse motion ([Miles](https://radixark.mintlify.app/diffusion/models/wan/wan2-2)). Fine detail, where photographic texture is decided, belongs to the low-noise expert. That is exactly where Flow-GRPO's maintainers warn that log-probability errors are largest ([flow_grpo](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo)). The low-noise expert should therefore be trained in one of three ways: with a likelihood-free objective, with DPO, or with truncated reward backpropagation on small differentiable rewards such as the photographic probe and identity similarity. Seedance 1.0 reportedly preferred this direct-reward route over DPO, PPO and GRPO ([Seedance 1.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09113v1)). The per-expert recommendation is an inference that no paper has yet tested.

### Cheaper levers come before full-scale RL

Budgets should be set honestly. The reference Wan 2.1 video-RL configuration runs at **240×416 pixels and 33 frames** ([flow_grpo config](https://github.com/yifan123/flow_grpo/blob/main/config/grpo.py)). The Flash-GRPO authors put a 14B experiment at "hundreds of GPU days", and DDRL's program consumed over a million GPU-hours.

Two lower-risk levers come before full RL. One is preference training of a super-resolution or refiner stage, which Seedance ran as a separate stage. The other is a GRPO-trained prompt enhancer, part of a 2026 post-training pipeline that reported about 31% better overall good-same-bad scores ([Systematic Post-Train](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25427)).

Test-time search complements RL rather than replacing it. Best-of-N multiplies serving cost by N for every clip, while RL's cost is paid once. Because Wan is open, white-box search is available for hero shots. EvoSearch, for example, keeps improving with compute where baselines plateau ([EvoSearch](https://hyper.ai/fr/papers/2505.17618)). For the future multi-shot model no multi-shot GRPO paper exists, so per-shot partitioned rewards plus cross-shot identity rewards are the closest guidance.

## The loop stays honest only behind gates it cannot see

### Four predictable hacks target the product's promise

Reward hacking is the expected outcome of optimizing generative video against learned scores, and the documented modes line up with this product's weak points.

Motion collapses. Annotators and reward models both prefer slow, artifact-free clips ([DenseDPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03517)). In one 2026 study, supervised fine-tuning dropped VBench dynamic degree **from 0.67 to 0.33, "near-static video generation"** ([SHIFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17426v2)). Meituan needed a dedicated motion reward to offset the static tendency that an image-aesthetic reward induced ([LongCat-Video](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22200v2)).

Looks over-stylize. Direct reward optimization oversaturated colors and did worse than the base model on realism ([SRPO](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2509.06942)). DDRL lists "over-stylization, or reduced diversity" among the standard symptoms ([DDRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04332)). For this product, the version that matters is a glossy "clean CG" look, which would break the core constraint.

Faces freeze. An ArcFace identity reward on Wan 2.2 produced stiff, expressionless "copy-paste" faces. Removing either the KL term or the multi-view scoring pool pushed the hacking rate above 50% ([IPRO](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Shen_Identity-Preserving_Image-to-Video_Generation_via_Reward-Guided_Optimization_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)).

Framing turns literal: a composition reward will favor centered, box-perfect framing over cinematic composition. The judge itself can also be steered by text in frames or in its context. A single opener such as "Thought process:" drew false-positive rewards from LLaMA3-70B **73% of the time** (secondary figure) ([One Token to Fool](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08794v1)).

These hacks also operate one level up, where no study has measured them. A prompt-strategy bandit with LLM reflection is exactly an upstream policy optimizing against a downstream VLM judge. If the judge rewards artifact-free takes, the bandit will drift toward locked-off cameras and slow push-ins. Best-of-N selection is optimization too. In the canonical LLM study, gold reward under best-of-n rises and then falls as optimization pressure grows ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)).

The defenses that work are combinations. The strongest stack pairs an estimator fix (ratio normalization) with a real-data anchor (forward KL), per-reward normalization with pairwise rewards, a KL penalty applied only to the roughly 10% of samples on which a reward ensemble is most uncertain ([GARDO](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24138v1)), explicit motion and diversity terms, and multi-view identity scoring. Diversity must be measured perceptually. On flow models, policy entropy **stays flat while perceptual diversity collapses**, because the noise schedule is fixed ([PEC](https://xiaofeng-tan.github.io/projects/PEC/)). Ensembles mitigate hacking but do not eliminate it, since their members share errors ([Eisenstein et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09244)). Motion, safety and the photographic look should be constraints rather than weights, held below the point where over-optimization begins ([Moskovitz et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04373)). At least one selection verifier should never be used as a training reward.

### Promotion runs on blind pairs, PPI and non-inferiority guardrails

Every change should be promoted only through a gate its author cannot see. That covers a new strategy, a judge rubric, a weight set or a Wan checkpoint. Google's Veo 3 card describes "assurance evaluations ... developed and run by a group outside of the model development team" ([Veo 3 model card](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Veo-3-Model-Card.pdf)). The app's equivalent is a frozen, stratified suite that the reflection call, the bandit and the judge-prompt optimizer never see. It should span dialogue close-ups, action, crowds, low light, multi-character identity and photographic-surreal shots, across all targets.

Candidates face blind pairwise human comparison against the incumbent, on the same storyboard frame and, where the provider exposes one, the same seed. The comparison is analyzed with Bradley–Terry and at least 1,000 bootstrap resamples, and a candidate passes only if the lower bound of its net win rate exceeds zero.

Prediction-powered inference (PPI) makes this affordable. A small human-labeled set measures and corrects the judge's systematic error on a large judge-labeled set. The result is an unbiased estimate whose variance shrinks with every judge label ([PPI](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05308)), and stratification tightens the intervals further ([Stratified PPI](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c9fcd02e6445c7dfbad6986abee53d0d-Abstract.html)). Roughly 100–300 human pairs per decision is a reasonable starting point, though it has not been validated.

Guardrails with non-inferiority margins catch what win rates miss: motion magnitude, perceptual diversity, the rate of CG-looking takes, the spread of identity similarity (a collapse in its variance signals copy-paste faces), safety-filter and provider-refusal rates, and judge–human agreement per dimension. Rollout then proceeds from shadow to canary to full ramp under always-valid sequential tests. Repeatedly peeking at fixed-horizon tests inflates false positives, a practice Kohavi and colleagues call "one of the most common mistakes in practical A/B testing" ([Johari et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.04922v1)). When many strategies compete, the false-discovery rate needs to be controlled. A 5–10% frozen-baseline holdout measures cumulative gains and vendor drift. A monthly blind refresh of 30–50 scenes, about 200 comparisons with four raters, is enough to see effects of roughly 60/40.

### Continual learning needs fresh reference data and conservative exploration

Continual learning adds slower failure modes. When users curate a system's own outputs and those outputs feed training, curation acts as implicit preference optimization that **amplifies the reward model's biases** ([Ferbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09499v1)). A NeurIPS 2026 analysis shows iterative curation driving the system toward one of several "singleton equilibria" unless reference data is injected at a sufficient rate ([Co-evolving preferences](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/148960)). Wan should therefore never train only on in-app winners. Every training round needs exploration traffic and a fixed share of real live-action footage. KL from the base model predicts forgetting in RL fine-tuning ([RL's Razor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04259)), so it should be tracked as an early warning alongside a test slice that checks retained capabilities.

For the bandit, conservative Thompson sampling keeps per-round performance near a baseline while playing the baseline only O(log T) times ([SCLTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00081v1)). In practice that means a traffic floor and a stop-loss for the incumbent strategy, parent posteriors shrunk when strategies are rewritten, and a cap on live arms. It also means reflection on fixed windows with a minimum sample size. Thompson sampling's tolerance of delayed, batched feedback makes such windows safe ([Chapelle & Li](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2011/hash/e53a0a2978c28872a4505bdb51db06dc-Abstract.html)).

### Poisoned votes and injected instructions need architectural defenses

Small amounts of bad feedback steer models. Injecting **1–5% poisoned preference pairs** steered an LLM toward a target entity and sentiment ([Best-of-Venom](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05530v2)). "Clean-label" attacks, which poison through correctly labeled but deliberately chosen examples, corrupted text-to-image reward models without any control over annotation ([BadReward](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03234v1)). Optimized injected sequences hijacked LLM judges, and perplexity and known-answer detection proved insufficient against them ([JudgeDeceiver](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17710)).

The app has five specific exposures: rating spam that promotes a strategy or a borderline style; instructions planted in story text or free-text feedback that reach the reflection or planning calls; on-screen text in generated frames, such as signage a provider renders, that steers the vision judge; prompt words like "photorealistic" in the judge's context that inflate its scores; and poisoned A/B choices entering Wan's training data.

The defenses must be architectural. Feedback handling needs per-account influence caps, reputation weights, gold pairs and Dawid–Skene-style rater-reliability models. It also needs anomaly detection on rating velocity and co-rating patterns, and quarantine of new or low-reputation accounts from model-level training data, with a noise-tolerant loss such as robust DPO ([rDPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00409)).

The reflection call should receive aggregate statistics plus delimited, quarantined excerpts of user text. That technique, "spotlighting", cut attack success from over 50% to under 2% in Microsoft's tests ([Spotlighting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14720)). The call should emit only a constrained schema checked by a policy linter, following the plan-then-execute and dual-LLM patterns ([Design patterns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08837)). The judge should see the pre-generation shot spec rather than prompt text, flag unrequested text that OCR finds in frames, and sit in a committee spanning model families.

### Safety, likeness and provenance sit outside the reward

Safety cannot be a weight that taste can trade away. Safety classifiers and likeness checks should run before a take is shown, rated or logged. Unsafe or non-consensual takes should be excluded from bandit rewards and RL positives and kept only in a separate safety dataset, with violations treated as a hard constraint in the manner of Safe RLHF ([Safe RLHF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12773)). The learner must not be rewarded for evading provider moderation. If "generation succeeded" enters the reward, the bandit and reflection loop can learn phrasings that slip past Seedance, Veo, Kling or Sora filters. Refusals should therefore count as neutral, and strategies should be linted for evasion patterns.

Real-person likeness should follow Sora 2's model: opt-in "characters" that owners control and can revoke, with consent attestation for image-to-video of real people ([FutureFive](https://futurefive.co.nz/story/openai-details-sora-2-safeguards-on-likeness-teens)). A lineage index should ensure that revocation also purges derived preference pairs, embeddings and training shards. Provider policy can change abruptly, as when ByteDance pledged new safeguards after Seedance 2.0's celebrity-deepfake backlash ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/02/17/bytedance-says-it-will-add-safeguards-to-ai-video-tool-seedance-20-following-hollywood-bac)). The planner must therefore degrade gracefully when references are refused. Upstream C2PA and watermark marks should be preserved through editing, and a fresh manifest signed at export.

Regulation now binds. EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties have applied since 2 August 2026. The voluntary Code of Practice published on 10 June 2026 sets two-layer marking with secured metadata and watermarking ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)). The Digital Omnibus reportedly gives systems already on the market until 2 December 2026 to comply with marking, though sources disagree on the details ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/publication/digital-omnibus-on-ai-adjusted-timelines-and-transitional-provisions-explained)). Serving its own fine-tuned Wan would plausibly make the app a provider under Article 50(2), with no grace period if launched after 2 August 2026; that is an inference, not legal advice. Photographic-surreal footage that resembles real people or events will often trigger deployers' deepfake-labelling duty, though duties are lighter for artistic and fictional work. In the United States, the TAKE IT DOWN Act's 48-hour removal duty has been enforced since 19 May 2026, with civil penalties up to **$53,088 per violation** ([WilmerHale](https://wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260615-the-take-it-down-act-goes-live)). It applies as soon as users can share outputs inside the app.

## Conclusion

The most consequential decision is sequencing: train the judge before training the generator. Every downstream learner inherits the reward stack's errors and amplifies them, whether it is the bandit, reflection, director memory or Wan RL. The comparisons needed for a decision fall about ninefold as judge agreement rises from 60% to 80%. Effort spent on agreement therefore compounds across every learner in a way a faster RL objective does not. L3 should not consume rewards until they pass the app's own pairwise benchmark. The photographic gate is the best single investment because it serves twice: as the product's guarantee, and as the barrier that most documented hacks would otherwise cross. The app also holds an advantage most video products lack. Frames-first planning makes every approved storyboard frame a matched context for paired comparison and every blueprint a verifiable composition reward, which turns ordinary use into well-controlled experiments.

The durable asset is consented, on-policy evidence: Wan's own rollouts, the gold program and licensed live-action footage. The cross-model log's value lies in calibration and routing rather than training. Much remains unmeasured. No public study tests whether a prompt-strategy learner can game a VLM judge in video. No benchmark scores photographic-versus-CGI look or storyboard adherence. No reward-hacking data exists for the hosted models, and no published benchmark covers an Opus-class Claude judging video. Most 2026 figures cited here are author-reported, several only through secondary summaries. Because the app's design naturally produces the right data, it is well placed to generate that missing evidence itself.
